# The performance axis, answered — 2026-08-07

Status: strategy record. This document answers the standing question "where
can Titen win on real, measurable performance, checkable by someone who does
not trust us" against the evidence in
[`EVALS.md`](../testing/EVALS.md), [`PONYTAIL-DEBT.md`](../../PONYTAIL-DEBT.md),
the [2026-08-07 landscape survey](./2026-08-07-memory-agent-landscape.md), and
the 2026-08-04 benchmark programme. Method: four independent proposals from
different vantage points, adversarial critique of every merged candidate
against five criteria (matters to a user; externally-authored data; nobody
measures it; structural favour; informative negative), then a judged
synthesis. Four candidates were killed on verified facts; their dispositions
are preserved below so they are not re-proposed. The measurement this document
selects is pre-registered in
[2026-08-07-pooled-store-prereg.md](../testing/2026-08-07-pooled-store-prereg.md)
and executed by the paired spec/plan `2026-08-07-pooled-store-benchmark`.

One version note: the candidate texts below name "Titen 0.6.0" because the
published per-instance numbers were produced by 0.6.0. The measurement itself
runs the public npm artifact `titen-memory@0.7.0`, whose two intervening
ranking-adjacent changes were each measured byte-identical at n=500 on this
corpus; the prereg's anchor gate enforces that expectation.

# Final answer: the performance axis, judged against the candidates and the repo's own evidence

**Verdict up front.** Four of the five merged candidates die on verified facts. One survives — weakened, and only after adopting its critique's repairs. The honest headline is therefore conditional, and it is this:

> **The de-saturated quality–cost frontier: what each memory system delivers, and what building its store costs, when LongMemEval-S is scored against one pooled 19,829-session store instead of ~50 sessions per instance — the condition EVALS.md itself proposed (#267) and nobody, vendor or academic, has ever run.** Titen's structural favor is on the cost coordinate and on owning the instrument, not on a promised recall win — and day 1 of the smallest experiment decides whether the axis exists at all.

Every load-bearing fact below was re-verified against the repository before being asserted: `docs/testing/EVALS.md`, `PONYTAIL-DEBT.md`, `docs/research/2026-08-04-memory-agent-landscape.md`, `docs/testing/2026-08-04-scale-and-concurrency.md`, `docs/testing/2026-08-07-durability.md`, `src/core/observations.ts`, `src/core/claims.ts`, `src/core/retrieval.ts`, and `gh issue view 267` (CLOSED 2026-08-05, the pooled run never executed).

## 1. The axis

Points of recall@1 a memory system loses when the candidate pool grows from the published demo condition (~50 sessions per instance) to the full pooled corpus (19,829 distinct sessions — ~400x), paired per-question with the measured cost of building that store (CPU-seconds, LLM calls, embedding calls, dollars). Unit: the **(recall@1 at pool size P, store-build cost)** pair per lane, plus **points-lost-per-decade** across nested pools P = 50 / 1,000 / 5,000 / 19,829, with MRR@10 and tokens-to-answer beside it per the repository's standing metric rule. The claim shape is deliberately **not** a recall win — that race is measured as saturated (13.4 points of spread at k=1 collapses to 2.2 at k=10; every serious lane inside noise). It is **non-inferiority within a pre-declared margin at 60x–3,000x lower measured build cost, at an operating point where no published number exists**. Framing, per the critique's repair: this is primarily an **instrument** — TREC-style pool enlargement that de-saturates a benchmark where every serious lane currently ties — and only secondarily a deployment story. The disclosed caveat ships in the pre-registration, not in a critic's rebuttal: the pooled store mixes 500 personas, so unscoped it models a shared org-fleet store, not a single user's; a **subject-scoped pooled arm** is run beside it so "real products scope by user_id" becomes a measured row instead of a free vendor rebuttal. A user cares because EVALS.md already predicts the mechanism: at realistic corpus sizes the k=1 saturation disappears on its own and **ranking, not candidate generation, becomes the binding constraint** — which is exactly the regime a store reaches after months of real use, and the regime in which "which system should I run, and what does its quality cost me" currently has no answer anywhere. This is Finding A, named as such per the brief's instruction; the non-obvious execution is the pooling protocol (single-subject ingest, strict-prefix nested decades, cross-instance contamination policy, anchor-cell reproduction gate) and the cost co-metric that explains why no incumbent will run it first.

## 2. Why it is open

- **The condition was verified unrun when this document was written, and was
  executed the same day.** The run is
  [2026-08-07-pooled-store.md](../testing/2026-08-07-pooled-store.md); two of
  its five pre-registered falsifiers fired against Titen. This bullet is kept
  as the state of the world the axis was chosen from, not as current fact.
  EVALS.md documents the shape — 19,829 distinct sessions across 25,112 (instance, session) pairs, median per-instance haystack 50 (min 39, max 66) — proposes the pooled condition explicitly as "better fix, future work," and tracks it as #267. #267 was **closed 2026-08-05 without the run ever executing**; the k=1/MRR primary-metric rule was adopted instead.
- **Every public harness structurally cannot reach it.** LongMemEval's own protocol, Mem0's harness, MemDelta's controlled baselines, and this repository's controlled-comparison contract (`reset → ingest → wait_ready → search_or_context`) all bake one-fresh-store-per-instance into the ingestion loop. Pooling requires rebuilding the harness, not re-running it. VectorDBBench-class suites measure ANN latency/recall over embeddings, not memory-system quality on conversational data.
- **The incumbents are commercially disincentivized.** Mem0's default-mode ingest measured 2,981 LLM calls and 288,021 summed seconds for 60 instances; the pooled store costs it roughly 20,000 LLM calls, and publishing any pooled number retroactively reframes its platform-only 94.4 demo-condition headline (a concession already verbatim in its README).
- **The only scale-vs-recall curve anywhere is Titen's own synthetic one** (recall@1 1.00 → 0.49 from 10^3 to 10^5 claims), which by its own admission does not transfer in intercept — only in shape. No externally-authored number describes the de-saturated regime.

## 3. Why Titen is favoured — honestly scoped

The favor is on the **cost half of the frontier and on position**, not on recall, and the critique's strongest objection — that the genuinely new number (pooled recall) is exactly the coordinate where Titen holds no structural favor — is absorbed into the claim shape rather than denied.

- **Architectural, cost:** Titen's write path performs zero per-item model inference — measured 816 s / 0 LLM calls / 0 embedding calls for all 246,930 turns at n=500 (~3.3 ms/turn). Rebuilding the pooled store costs ~11 minutes and $0, on any host, forever. Mem0 OSS 2.0.15 `infer=False` needed 14,827 summed seconds and 29,582 embedding calls at n=60 and cannot run without an embedding provider; its default `infer=True` is ~350x the control's ingest. Per-item inference on the write path **is** their product thesis (extraction for Mem0, the embedding store for MemPalace), so closing the cost gap means shipping and headlining a no-inference mode — conceding MemDelta's finding against their own marketing.
- **Positional, honestly restated per the critique:** the durable asset is **not** that competitors cannot run the pooled protocol — a funded team can contest it in weeks for hundreds of dollars once published. It is that Titen re-runs it at **$0 marginal cost per release**, pre-registers, and publishes losses (three public retractions on record). That is a **cadence-and-credibility position, not a moat**, and the answer says so. The pooled harness ships with runnable Mem0/MemPalace adapters in the pre-registration, so the protocol is shared before anyone can call it vendor-shaped.
- **Neutralization estimate:** a rival pooled *number* — weeks, once the protocol is public (Mem0 can run `infer=False` pooled privately and publish selectively; MemPalace re-runs for one overnight). A rival at Titen's *cost point* — shipping a headlined zero-inference lexical mode — one to two quarters and a strategy reversal. The recall coordinate is defended by nothing and the design does not pretend otherwise.
- **One internal contradiction must be resolved before any competitor cost is quoted:** the repo carries MemPalace ingest at both 486 s (n=500, MiniLM, user-only — ~2 ms/turn, implying a ~8-minute pooled rebuild) and 6,065.8 s (n=60 vector lane — ~0.21 s/turn, implying ~11 hours pooled). Day 0 re-measures MemPalace per-turn ingest on a 100-session sample and the pre-registration quotes only that.

## 4. Measurement design, falsifier first

**Pre-registered before any pooled query runs**, as `docs/testing/2026-08-07-pooled-store-prereg.md` (same pattern as the 2026-08-07 prereg documents). Falsifiers, written now:

1. **Anchor gate.** The P=50 anchor cell must reproduce the published n=500 numbers within ±0.002 recall@1 per lane, or the harness is broken and nothing else is reported.
2. **Axis-existence falsifier.** If no serious lane loses ≥2 points of recall@1 from P=50 to P=19,829, pool size does not matter on real conversational data at this corpus's maximum size. Finding A's premise fails on external data; the null is published, the axis retired, and EVALS.md's #267 reasoning corrected in print.
3. **Frontier falsifier.** If Titen FTS-only at P=19,829 falls more than 10 points of recall@1 below the best zero-LLM control lane, or its paired scale tax exceeds the dense control's by ≥5 points (sign test, p<0.05), the zero-provider operating point does not survive the de-saturated shape. The frontier claim is dead; the result is published as deployment guidance ("vectors required above ~10^3 sessions") and the product story retreats to the hybrid lane.
4. **No-relative-claim outcome.** If all serious lanes' taxes are statistically indistinguishable, there is no lead — only a field-first absolute degradation curve, publishable but claimed as exactly that.

**Corpus and protocol.** Full LongMemEval-S (MIT, externally authored, 500 questions, 246,930 turns). All 19,829 distinct sessions ingested into **one single-subject store per lane** (single org, single subject, so the candidate pool is genuinely pooled — Titen's FTS `MATCH` conjoins `org_scope`/`subject_scope` by design, verified in `src/core/retrieval.ts`). Nested decades P = 50 / 1,000 / 5,000 / 19,829 selected by deterministic hash of session ID, each decade a strict prefix of the next, every gold session always included; seed-pinned. **Two query arms:** unscoped-pooled (the new cell) and subject-scoped pooled (the existing published condition on the 424,168-claim store, kept as the measured answer to "just scope by user_id" — what scoping buys, and what it cannot buy when the pool is one subject's own year of history).

**Lanes, every configuration named:** Titen 0.6.0 FTS-only; Titen 0.6.0 FTS+vector (local ollama embedder, model pinned and fingerprinted); verbatim-RAG control on the **same pinned embedder** (closes the MemDelta embedding-confound door), both embedding configs; MemPalace 3.6.0 (MiniLM, user-only, the configuration that scored 0.804 at n=500); Mem0 OSS 2.0.15 `infer=False` as phase 2. Mem0 `infer=True` is **never a scored lane** — its pooled cost is reported as a clearly-labeled extrapolation from its measured per-turn figures, and the asymmetry (one lane in the table cannot afford to be measured at production shape on one machine) is itself a published result. No managed-platform comparison anywhere, per the standing claim-discipline rule.

**Scoring.** Session-ID gold (`answer_session_ids`), one shared scorer, failures kept in the denominator, recall@1 and MRR@10 primary (standing rule), paired per-question sign tests. **Dual-scored:** strict session-ID, and audit-adjusted — every rank-1 miss that retrieved a cross-instance session is hand-audited on a fixed n=50 sample, the fraction genuinely containing the answer is published, and the raw audit sample ships for third-party rejudging (the residual bias direction plausibly favors the lexical lane; disclosing it is what keeps the run honest). Per-lane build cost instrumented from `/proc` (CPU-s), call counters, wall clock. Per-question-type breakdown published. Raw JSONL + SHA256SUMS in `results/`, per the reproducibility record.

## 5. The smallest experiment

**3–4 days, one person, entirely inside `~/titen-bench-20260804/` on `benchmark-host`** (corpus, lane runners, competitor venvs, local ollama, prior artifacts `results/*-500.json` all present; 16 threads / 30 GB suffice — a ~424k-claim single-file store is ~2 GB by the measured ~4.8 KB/pair, RSS stays ~100 MiB).

- **Day 0 (half day).** Publish the pre-registration: falsifiers above, tolerances, lane configurations, contamination policy, decade hash seed. Re-measure MemPalace per-turn ingest on a 100-session sample to resolve 486 s vs 6,065.8 s before quoting any competitor cost. Publish the pooled harness diff plus Mem0/MemPalace adapter configs with it.
- **Day 1 — decisive either way.** Build the single-subject pooled Titen store (~11–15 min ingest, $0). Run the P=50 anchor (must reproduce 0.880/0.9147) and Titen FTS-only at all four decades, both query arms (~15 min for 500 compiles; compile p50 was 11.4 ms at 10^5 claims). **If Titen FTS-only pooled holds near 0.85, the frontier exists and everything after merely prices it; if it craters toward 0.5, falsifier 3 fires before a single competitor lane runs and two days are saved.** Start embedding 19,829 sessions on local ollama overnight for the control and the vector arm.
- **Day 2.** Score verbatim-RAG control and Titen FTS+vector at all decades; paired sign tests.
- **Day 3.** MemPalace 3.6.0 pooled ingest (+run; ~8 minutes or overnight depending on the day-0 re-measure), Mem0 OSS 2.0.15 `infer=False` pooled (~34 summed hours at 0.50 s/turn, parallelized across 16 workers ≈ overnight; ~30k local embedding calls, $0).
- **Day 4.** Decade curves, contamination audit (n=50, published raw), per-type breakdown, dual scoring, write-up with each pre-registered falsifier's verdict stated explicitly.

Every branch pays: a field-first degradation curve with a priced frontier, a published inversion ("vectors required above N sessions"), or a null that retires Finding A on external data and corrects the repository's own #267 reasoning in print.

## 6. The strongest argument against this answer

Three attacks, none fully dischargeable. **(a) The novelty and the favor are disjoint.** The only genuinely new number is pooled recall — the coordinate where Titen holds no architectural advantage, where its own synthetic 1.00→0.49 slope (driven by 250 engineered same-topic competitors per query, far denser than real conversations) predicts it may lose outright, and which any funded competitor can contest in weeks once the protocol is public. The structurally-unassailable coordinate — build cost — is already published ground (MemDelta §4.4, the repo's own ingest columns, PONYTAIL-DEBT names it "the defensible claim"), and its pooled value nearly follows from arithmetic. The answer absorbs this by claiming only non-inferiority-at-cost and instrument ownership; a critic can still fairly say that is Finding A plus the known cost story, executed carefully — the brief permits exactly that, if named, and it is named. **(b) The likeliest single outcome is the null.** ~2×10^4 real, lexically-separable conversations may simply not de-saturate anything; falsifier 2 fires, and no larger externally-authored conversational corpus exists to escalate to. Then the published result is "no such axis exists at measurable scale" — informative, but the end of the positioning question, not a step in it. **(c) External validity is squeezed from both directions.** Other personas' histories are easy distractors, so the unscoped tax may understate the true single-user tax (one subject's self-similar year) while overstating the scoped-fleet one; the scoped arm and the disclosed caveat bound this but cannot remove it — and a vendor can still say, correctly, that no shipped product serves the unscoped query pattern, including Titen, whose authorization-before-retrieval design exists to prevent it.

## Ranking, and what to run first

**Run first: the axis above.** It is the cheapest experiment that can kill or found the whole positioning, it reuses the existing lanes nearly unchanged, day 1 alone is decisive, its outcome fixes the store size every later measurement should run at, and every falsifier branch is publishable under the project's discipline.

**Run second, as a measurement program, not a lead axis: the repaired closed-loop write-back study (Finding B).** The original candidate dies as a "Titen structurally wins" axis on verified facts: Titen's outcome is decidable from source without running anything (`recalled` is server-stamped fail-closed in `src/core/observations.ts`, refused as claim evidence in `src/core/claims.ts`, and ranking is claims-FTS-only — the dose-response curve is provably flat); a cooperative single-caller harness cannot distinguish that enforced provenance from a competitor's advisory `metadata={'origin':'retrieved'}` tag plus a search filter, which Mem0's shipped OSS API already accepts — a cookbook page, not the claimed one-to-two quarters; `context_token` is threaded nowhere in `src/sdk.ts` or the MCP surface (grep-verified: core, `api.md`, and tests only), so "Titen as documented" is today the unprotected arm; and the code's own comment concedes the stamp is a lower bound (proves the write was made while holding a pack, not that its content came from it) and is per-observation, so a mixed echo-plus-new write is wholly stamped and can never rank — the cheat-closing condition boomerangs. **What survives repair and is worth running:** primary metric promoted to content-level harm — answer-string containment in the packed context across R=5 lossy write-back rounds (the one genuinely open empirical question: does multi-generation re-packing shear answers out and displace originals?) — with competitor hygiene lanes (metadata-tag + note-splitting cookbook, `infer=True` at full n on reused stores) as first-class arms from day one, and the headline as **cost and default-ness of equal hygiene**: zero calls and enforced-by-default versus caller homework or ~50 LLM calls per instance. Preconditions: ship `context_token` threading through the SDK and MCP surface first and scope the claim to that integration path; drop the mixed-payload condition or build span-level stamping. Falsifier: if patched competitor lanes match Titen's hygiene at zero measured cost, the write-contract story is published as a cookbook page; if no lane shows content-level harm at dose 100 after R=5, Finding B is published as a storage-cost story — the informative negative that corrects the field's read of the one production audit (whose 97.8%/10,134 figure the repo's own audit self-report already cites). 3–4 days, after the pooled run fixes the store size.

**Killed, with dispositions:**

- **Acknowledged-write survivability.** Killed: SIGKILL of a process or container never defeats the OS page cache — SQLite is application-crash-safe even at `synchronous=OFF` — so the headline metric reads zero for every system regardless of durability engineering, the falsifier fires vacuously, and acting on that null (dropping the measured 6.7x `synchronous=FULL` ingest tax) would be a measurement error. Salvage as **unpublished engineering diligence only**: a VM-level power-loss harness (qemu/firecracker, kill the VM so the guest page cache dies) is the only way to learn what the 6.7x tax buys — the durability report itself states "no crash-injection, no power-loss test; `synchronous = FULL` is trusted rather than demonstrated." Never a competitive axis: the end state is a converged all-zeros table quoting competitors' faster ingest from our own harness.
- **Claim-verification cost.** Killed twice by the brief's own text: it measures the evidence pipeline, not the system doing its job, and it matches the banned vendor-authored pattern (we script, we set tolerance, we adjudicate). Its factual premise also fails on the repo's own n=500 table: MemPalace's reproduction is already ~$0/no-key/486 s-class, and Titen's actual bolded headline (0.900 FTS+vector: 4,989 embedding calls, 13,702 s index drain) fails the axis's own under-an-hour/$0/exact test. Salvage: one day of packaging — ship `verify-benchmark.sh` for Titen's own published numbers as a claims-discipline artifact. Never publish it as a comparison table.
- **Embedding-migration drift.** Killed: the fingerprint half is a sprint-scale metadata tag for any competitor; the floor half is the banned retrieval race in a degraded costume (FTS-only 0.817 vs Mem0 0.833 at n=60, p=1.0) and its 0.880 figure is contradicted at exactly the mid-life scale the axis targets by the 1.00→0.49 curve; MemPalace's pinned local model never experiences the event at all. Salvage: a one-off published measurement note — "frozen-model protocols structurally cannot observe a model transition; here is what five systems serve during one" — with Titen as one row, not a race it claims to win.

## The honest meta-conclusion

Held to the brief's five criteria simultaneously, **no axis was found on which Titen is structurally favored to win a new quality number.** The pattern across all five candidates is uniform and verified: every genuinely unmeasured quality coordinate (pooled recall, closed-loop content harm, migration-window quality) is open precisely where Titen holds no architectural favor, and every coordinate where the architecture is genuinely unassailable — zero-inference write cost, $0 re-runs, deterministic outputs, enforced provenance — is either already published, derivable by arithmetic, or indistinguishable from an advisory tag under any cooperative-caller harness. Criteria 3 and 4 intersect only on the cost coordinate and on the discipline asset.

What survives is therefore a narrower, conditional claim, and it should be stated as such wherever it is published: **Titen can plausibly own the frontier — non-inferior quality at the de-saturated store shape at 60x–3,000x lower measured build cost — and it can certainly own the instrument**, re-measured per release for $0, pre-registered, losses included, with competitor adapters shipped alongside. If day 1 fires falsifier 3, the product story retreats to the hybrid lane and says so; if falsifier 2 fires, the published result is that the field's Finding A does not survive contact with the largest externally-authored corpus available — which is the "no such axis exists" conclusion, argued with a measurement instead of an opinion, and this document is its pre-registration of that possibility.
